The "Third Place" Concept

Strategic Relevance

The Starbucks experience relies heavily on human interaction. Staff management is not merely operational — it is a strategic determinant of service quality and customer perception.

Emotional Labouring
High-Contact Service
Emotional labour was first conceptualised by Hochschild (1983) as the management of feeling to create a publicly observable display.

In high-contact service environments, prolonged emotional dissonance increases burnout risk (Grandey, 2000).

Starbucks institutionalises emotional display as part of brand identity.

SERVICE-DOMINANT LOGIC
According to Service-Dominant Logic (Vargo & Lusch, 2004), value is co-created through interaction. As mobile ordering reduces interaction, Starbucks risks weakening value co-creation.
STANDARDISATION–ADAPTATION DILEMMA
Global service brands face the standardisation–adaptation dilemma (Levitt, 1983). Starbucks attempts a "glocal" strategy: global brand identity with local cultural integration.

🇯🇵 Japan: 'Omotenashi' + global standards  |  🇮🇹 Italy: Balance with local coffee culture
STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS
1

REDUCE TURNOVER

Action: Review peak-hour workloads, introduce 'recovery time' periods · Theory: Service Profit Chain → Employee satisfaction · Impact: +10% retention = £1.2M savings per 1,000 employees

2

REBALANCE SPEED AND EMPATHY

Action: Dedicated roles: Mobile Specialist vs Connection Barista · Theory: SERVQUAL → Empathy + Responsiveness · Impact: Improved NPS and customer satisfaction during peak hours

3

CONSTRUCTIVE DIALOGUE

Action: Active listening channels, co-create solutions with partners · Theory: Reduces emotional dissonance, improves Reliability · Impact: Lower reactive unionisation and stronger retention

✓ SUCCESS CRITERIA: Measure using eNPS and NPS, not solely financial KPIs
